Stingrays (or IMSI Catchers) are devices that impersonate a cellular tower and cause nearby phones to identify themselves. Police can use device identifiers and signal-strength information to locate or track a particular phone. The privacy problem is that these devices initially interact with every compatible phone in range, not only the target’s, potentially sweeping up identifiers or signaling data from hundreds of freakin innocent bystanders. What happens to that non-target information? whether it is immediately deleted, retained, searched, or reused is unknown. I guess it depends on the agency’s minimization, retention, and auditing safeguards which are usually left extremely vague on purpose, or they just don't exist at all.

In March 2026, Texas DPS issued a $4.49 million emergency purchase order for four Chevrolet Tahoes, four Cognyte FalcoNet cellular-tracking systems, and related equipment. This investigation examines what the technology can do, DPS’s vague claim that it was needed for “immediate law enforcement use,” internal reviews that began or concluded after the purchase order was sent to Cognyte, and unanswered questions about competition, oversight, and how information from innocent bystanders’ phones is handled.

## About this repository

This repository contains records released by the Texas Department of Public Safety (DPS) in response to a request under the Texas Public Information Act, Texas Government Code Chapter 552.

I submitted the request by email on August 12, 2026. DPS recorded it as received on August 13, 2026, under reference P022257-081326. The request sought records related to:

Purchase Order 26P0010314

Requisition 26R0014623

Cognyte Quote 1299

Contract 0000000000000000000005527

The Cognyte FalcoNet acquisition approved in March 2026

DPS delivered these records through its Public Information Center as a partial production at no cost. This archive documents that production; it should not be read as confirmation that DPS produced every responsive record requested.

---

## What the records contain

The records include the requisition and approval chain, emergency decision memorandum, purchase-order correspondence, CJIS and cybersecurity determinations, CRB transparency and attestation review threads, director approval, and a later Compliance and Quality Control review.

### Among other things, the records show:

The requisition is dated February 26, 2026 and completed its CAPPS approval chain on March 3.

DPS sent the purchase order to Cognyte at approximately 11:57-11:58 a.m. on March 4.

The emergency memorandum was approved on March 5. (purchase order was sent out a day before emergency memorandum was approved)

A CJIS request was expressly labeled an "Emergency Purchase, Post award review."

Later CRB correspondence identifies the lifecycle phase as "Post Award, CRB Review."

Cyber concluded on March 11 that Cyber, Data and AI, and IT exhibits were not required.

Compliance and Quality Control deemed the acquisition documents compliant on April 30 while recommending several file corrections, including addressing a SAM.gov check performed outside the stated seven-day timeframe.

These records establish the chronology of those particular reviews. <b> BUT They do not prove that no verbal authorization or other unproduced review occurred before the purchase order was transmitted. OKAY??? PLEASE DONT SUE. </b>
